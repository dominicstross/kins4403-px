Golden Rule: Lactate is Fuel, Free Protons (H+) Cause Acidosis

When you sprint, your fast-twitch fibers produce Lactate + H+. The H+ ion causes fatigue and burning—but the Lactate molecule is packed with chemical energy. Specialized door proteins called MCTs (Monocarboxylate Transporters) ship this lactate out into the blood to fuel your heart and liver!

Interactive Road Map

Step 1 Type II Sprint Muscle (Producer)

During hard exercise, fast glycolytic fibers generate ATP without oxygen, churning out Pyruvate which converts to Lactate. MCT4 transporters pump this lactate out into the bloodstream.

Step 2 Capillary Bloodstream Loop

Circulating blood carries lactate away from active sprinting muscles. This prevents local buildup and allows oxidative tissues around the body to grab it.

Route A Intracellular Shuttle (Type I & Heart)

Slow-twitch endurance fibers and the cardiac muscle drink in lactate using MCT1 doorways. They turn it back to pyruvate and burn it aerobically for +15 ATP per molecule!

Route B The Cori Cycle (Liver Salvage)

The liver pulls lactate from blood via MCT2. Through gluconeogenesis, hepatic enzymes spend energy to rebuild lactate back into fresh Glucose, replenishing muscle glycogen.
