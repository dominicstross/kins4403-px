Physiology & Clearance Reference

1. The Anaerobic / Lactate Threshold (LT & OBLA)

Under resting conditions and light exercise, the rate of lactate production ($R_a$) equals the rate of clearance ($R_d$), maintaining blood lactate between 1.0 and 2.0 mmol/L.

  • Lactate Threshold (LT): The inflection point where $R_a$ exceeds $R_d$, and blood lactate begins to rise exponentially above resting baseline. In untrained adults, this occurs at ~50–60% $\text{V̇O}_2\text{max}$. Endurance training shifts LT rightward to ~70–85% $\text{V̇O}_2\text{max}$.
  • OBLA (Onset of Blood Lactate Accumulation): Clinically defined as the point where blood lactate concentration crosses 4.0 mmol/L.

2. Clearance Pathways: Active vs. Passive Rest

Following intense fast glycolysis, lactate is cleared through two main routes:

  1. Aerobic Oxidation (~75%): Neighboring slow-twitch (Type I) skeletal muscle and myocardium import lactate via MCT1, convert it to pyruvate via LDH-H, and oxidize it in the Krebs cycle.
  2. The Cori Cycle (~25%): The liver extracts circulating lactate via MCT2 and resynthesizes glucose via hepatic gluconeogenesis (costing 6 ATP).

Why Active Recovery Wins: Light exercise (35%–45% $\text{V̇O}_2\text{max}$) elevates muscle blood flow and cardiac output without stimulating new fast glycolytic flux, almost doubling the clearance rate compared to lying down.

3. Buffering Adaptations & HIIT Prescription

High-intensity intervals lasting 30 to 90 seconds with work-to-rest ratios of 1:3 to 1:5 maximally stress the fast glycolytic system. Chronic adaptations include:

  • Upregulation of the intracellular dipeptide buffer carnosine and extracellular bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$).
  • Increased expression of MCT1 (uptake) and MCT4 (efflux) monocarboxylate transporters.
  • Delayed onset of Troponin C $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ competition and reduced PFK enzyme inhibition.
