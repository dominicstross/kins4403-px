The Key Step 6 Biochemical Insight

Why do muscles convert Pyruvate into Lactic Acid if it causes acidosis?
To regenerate NAD⁺! Without oxygen, NADH accumulates and stalls glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (Step 6 of glycolysis). The LDH reaction recycles NAD⁺ so ATP synthesis can continue during life-or-death explosive efforts.

Exercise Physiology Reference Guide

1. The Metabolic Fate of Pyruvate (3C)

Glycolysis cleaves 1 glucose molecule into two 3-carbon pyruvate molecules. Pyruvate’s destination depends strictly on exercise intensity and mitochondrial oxygen availability:

  • Slow (Aerobic) Glycolysis: Pyruvate crosses the double mitochondrial membrane into the matrix, where pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) converts it to Acetyl-CoA for the Krebs cycle.
  • Fast (Anaerobic) Glycolysis: When ATP demands surpass mitochondrial speed, pyruvate remains in the sarcoplasm and is reduced by lactate dehydrogenase (LDH).

Key Biochemical Equations

LDH Reduction Reaction: Pyruvate + NADH + H⁺ ⇌ Lactic Acid + NAD⁺
Instantaneous Sarcoplasmic Dissociation: Lactic Acid (C₃H₆O₃) ⇌ Lactate⁻ (C₃H₅O₃⁻) + H⁺
Net Result: NAD⁺ is salvaged for Step 6, but free H⁺ induces acidosis.

2. The 3 Mechanisms of Intramuscular Acidosis Fatigue

Lactate itself does not cause fatigue or the burning sensation. The culprit is free hydrogen ions (H⁺), which drop sarcoplasmic pH from 7.1 down to 6.45:

  1. Enzyme Inhibition: High [H⁺] allosterically inhibits phosphofructokinase (PFK), reducing glycolytic speed.
  2. Impaired Excitation-Contraction Coupling: H⁺ competes directly with Ca²⁺ for the binding site on Troponin C, attenuating actin-myosin cross-bridge force.
  3. Sensory Pain Stimulation: Free protons activate chemical nociceptors (pain sensory fibers) in the muscle bed, triggering the severe muscular "burn".

3. Untrained vs. Endurance-Trained Thresholds

In untrained individuals, mitochondrial density and capillary networks support aerobic sorting up to only 50–60% V̇O₂max. In endurance-trained athletes, heightened mitochondrial volume and monocarboxylate transporter (MCT) expression delay the anaerobic threshold until 75–85% V̇O₂max.
