The 3 Acidosis Mechanisms Guide

The Fallacy: "Lactic Acid Causes Muscle Fatigue"

At normal human intramuscular pH (~7.0), lactic acid has a pKa of 3.86. Therefore, >99.9% of lactic acid immediately dissociates into:

Lactic Acid → Lactate⁻ + H⁺

Lactate⁻ is a high-energy metabolic intermediate shuttled to the heart, slow-twitch fibers, and liver (Cori cycle). It does not block troponin, does not shut down PFK, and does not stimulate pain receptors.

1. Impaired Excitation-Contraction Coupling

Troponin C has specific coordinating carboxyl groups designed to bind Ca²⁺ released by the sarcoplasmic reticulum. When sarcoplasmic pH drops below 6.8, the astronomical surge in free H⁺ concentration causes direct competitive binding. When H⁺ occupies Troponin C:

  • Tropomyosin fails to move out of the myosin-binding groove on actin.
  • Myosin heads are physically blocked from attaching.
  • Active force and peak power output decline catastrophically.

2. Allosteric Inhibition of Phosphofructokinase (PFK)

PFK is the rate-limiting enzyme in glycolysis. Protons (H⁺) bind to allosteric inhibitory sites on PFK subunits, decreasing its affinity for Fructose-6-Phosphate. This halts fast glycolytic flux, drastically lowering the rate of ATP resynthesis during sustained high-intensity contractions.

3. Group III & IV Afferent Sensory Pain Firing

Free protons activate acid-sensing ion channels (ASIC3) and TRPV1 receptors on non-myelinated Group IV metaboreceptive sensory fibers. This produces the searing "muscle burn" signal transmitted via the spinothalamic tract to the brain, commanding the motor cortex to reduce central drive.
